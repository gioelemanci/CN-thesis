\starredchapter{Abstract}

The transition from a physiological inter-ictal state to an epileptic seizure is a complex dynamical process characterized by abrupt shifts in cerebral synchronization. This work investigates the pre-ictal to ictal transition in focal epilepsy using human stereo-electroencephalography (sEEG) recordings analyzed through the framework of Complex Network Theory and statistical mechanics. By constructing time-varying functional networks via zero-lag Pearson correlation without absolute value rectification ($W_{ij}(\tau) \in [-1, 1]$), we strictly separate cooperative in-phase synchronization from competitive counter-phase decoupling. Our multiscale methodology tracks the macroscopic percolation phase transition and topological susceptibility of the network, the mesoscopic collapse of modular community structures via Newman's modularity and the Louvain heuristic, and the microscopic functional reorganization of individual recording contacts. By evaluating node metrics across robust statistical epochs rather than noisy instantaneous frames, we introduce a two-dimensional Functional Role Map that classifies recording channels into premonitory initiators, secondary ictal amplifiers, and counter-phase poles. Finally, we demonstrate that purely topological network descriptors can effectively capture the critical symmetry breaking and functional architecture of the epileptogenic network, independent of three-dimensional Euclidean spatial coordinates.
